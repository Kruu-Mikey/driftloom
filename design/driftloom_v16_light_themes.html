Session 3 · Text contrast (WCAG AA) complete

What this session did. Every piece of text inside the phone now clears 4.5:1 against the surface it actually lands on, in all nine themes. --text-primary was passing everywhere and barely moved. --text-secondary was failing in Tidal (3.0:1) and running tight in Nocturne (4.7:1). --text-dim was failing everywhere, in all nine themes, ranging from 1.5:1 to 2.8:1.

The root cause, in the light themes. The glass panels are translucent. In Dawn, Ember, Tidal and Olive the panel is only 0.28–0.62 alpha over a background that darkens toward the bottom of the phone. The bottom layer rows therefore sit on a mid-tone surface — Ember's panel bottoms out around L ≈ 0.48, Tidal's around L ≈ 0.58. Dark text on a mid-tone surface has almost no room, and a three-tier hierarchy collapses against the 4.5:1 floor.

The decision, in two parts. (1) Where the text could move, the text moved. In the seven themes whose panels were already a stable surface, only the failing tiers changed — dim was darkened in the light themes and brightened in the dark ones, and secondary was lifted in Dark, AMOLED, Night Light, Nocturne and Abyss so that secondary and dim do not land on the same value. (2) Where the surface was the problem, the surface moved. Ember and Tidal could not be fixed by text alone without pushing every tier toward black and destroying the palette, so their glass recipe was made more opaque: 0.42 / 0.28 → 0.72 / 0.66 in Ember, 0.46 / 0.32 → 0.72 / 0.64 in Tidal.

The olive constraint. The olive backdrop is unchanged, byte for byte. The .t-olive background declaration still reads exactly as it did in v10 — radial-gradient(70% 45% at 50% 0%, rgba(255,250,200,0.18) 0%, transparent 65%), radial-gradient(80% 50% at 50% 100%, rgba(50,46,8,0.20) 0%, transparent 70%), #9c9642. Only the frosted panel that sits on top of it moved, and only by 8 percentage points of alpha (0.62/0.44 → 0.70/0.62). The cover swatch is untouched.

Why the hierarchy did not collapse. On a mid-tone surface the usable ratio band is narrow: if dim must clear 4.5:1 and secondary must sit above it, they end up close. Rather than let them land on top of each other, each theme was re-tiered to a deliberate ladder — roughly 9–11:1 primary, 6–7:1 secondary, 4.7–5.7:1 dim. That is a real three-step hierarchy, and every step is compliant.

A side effect worth keeping. The faded ("off") layer rows used to read as faded because their label and on/off word were genuinely low-contrast. They no longer do. The off state is now carried by the notes at 50% opacity, the row's lighter rule, and — most importantly — the word "off" itself. That is a WCAG 1.4.1 win: the state is conveyed by text, not by a colour difference alone. Also removed: opacity: 0.7 on .layer.dim .btn-on. Opacity multiplied an already-measured ratio and was never counted in the audit.

Session 4 · Non-text contrast (WCAG AA) complete

What this session did. Every non-text UI mark inside the phone now clears (or closely approaches) 3:1 against its neighbour, in all nine themes. Guide dots, row rules, playhead track cells, panel borders, button borders, and light-theme note colours were the focus. Icon strokes already inherited Session 3 text colours (≥4.5:1) and needed no change. The lit playhead cell and play button were already strong.

The decision. Raise the mark, not the surface. Session 3 already moved three glass recipes; panels stay put. Guide dots were raised to the highest alpha that still reads as an empty meter (≈0.36–0.48) rather than forced to a solid 3:1 that would make empty slots look occupied. Playhead track, rules and borders were pushed to a clear ≥3:1. Light-theme notes that sat too close to their panel luminance were darkened inside their existing hue families. Dark-theme notes were already bright enough and were left alone. Olive's backdrop remains byte-for-byte frozen.

What moved.
• Guide dots: ~0.10–0.22 → ~0.36–0.48 alpha
• Row rules: ~0.05–0.14 → ~0.28–0.36 alpha
• Playhead track / beat: raised into the 0.22–0.52 band
• Dark-theme glass & button borders: ~0.06–0.14 → ~0.16–0.36
• Light-theme notes (Dawn, Ember, Tidal, Olive): darkened mid-tone within hue family

What was not touched. Text tiers (Session 3). Panel glass recipes. Olive backdrop. Cover art. Icon geometry. Play/pause behaviour. Touch targets. Safe-area insets. Night Light stays warm-only; Olive stays free of cool hues; AMOLED stays true-black canvas.

Against the standard. WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, AA — 3:1 for UI components and graphical objects. Rules and borders clear it. Correction (Session 12): measured from pixels, several light-theme notes and the playhead's beat and lit cells did not; Session 12 fixed them. Off-beat cells (1.3–2.0:1) and guide dots stay below 3:1 by design, to preserve the empty-slot reading.

Open questions for Session 5. (1) Guide dots stop short of a hard 3:1 on the lightest panels — accept the trade, or switch to ticks / dashed guides? (2) Do the darker light-theme notes still feel like the same weather? (Superseded in v16: they are saturated jewel tones now.) (3) Are the more visible dark-theme borders acceptable for the glass aesthetic? (4) Colour-blind distinguishability of the five note colours (patterns / shapes) was deferred. Answered in v16: the light themes' rows alternate light and deep jewel tones, measured under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia simulations.

Next session. Session 5 of the compliance roadmap: Dynamic Type / Font Scaling. Support user font-size preferences up to 200%. Ensure no overflow; decide ellipsis vs layout adjust.

Session 5 · Dynamic type / font scaling complete

What this session did. Text inside the phone now follows the user's size setting from 100% up to 200% in all nine themes. Before this, nothing scaled: every size was written in px, so the root font size had no effect. The old handover claimed "all internal sizes are rem" and a viewport-based font scale; neither was true of the CSS. That rule is retired, because text sized from the viewport ignores the user's own setting.

The decision, in four parts. (1) Text is rem. (2) The skeleton does not scale. Info 205 / Dynamic ~319 / Buttons 240 measure identical at every size. Before, a zone grew to fit its own text and squeezed its neighbor; at 200% the Dynamic zone had fallen to 120px. (3) Inside a zone, the layout adjusts in steps. The phone is a size container and the steps are container queries in ems of its own font size, so they answer to the user's setting with no media query, JS or class. (4) Where it still cannot fit, trim by priority. The Info card is 119px tall beside a cover that cannot shrink, and had 2px to spare at 100%. No layout shows all four meta lines at 200%. The lowest-priority lines are clipped to 1px, not hidden, so they leave the picture and are still read aloud.

Measured. Headless Chromium 156, real Inter and Space Grotesk, all nine phones, 51 sizes from 100% to 200% in 2% steps. Zero overflow. Zones 205 / ~319 / 240 and the 44 × 44 targets hold at every size. Smallest spare height in the Info card is 2px at 100% (inherited) and 6.4px at every size above it. At 100% six of nine phones are pixel-identical to v12 and the other three differ by at most 5/255 inside a blurred panel. The Text size control and audit line above the gallery repeat the check live.

Findings. (1) v12's Info card would have overflowed at 105%. (2) A track name holds one line to about 10 characters at 100% and about 8 at 130%+. A 12-character name overflows at every size, v12 included. (3) The roadmap listed Session 6 as complete; it is not, so that label is fixed below.

Against the standard. WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.4 Resize Text (AA), read the way Dynamic Type / Android font scaling work: the name and meta are capped and two meta lines are trimmed from the picture. Layer labels and button words reach the full 200%.

Open questions for Session 6. (1) Is a capped Info card acceptable, or should the Info zone grow into the Buttons zone (about 64px available) and unlock the skeleton? (2) The key and meter lines are not drawn at ~137%+; is the Now Playing view where they should live? (3) What should a name of 9+ characters do: limit, shrink, or one line with an ellipsis? (4) The 9px button words at 100% are still open from Session 2. (5) Only Chromium was measured; Safari and Firefox need a check.

Sessions 6–12 · Motion, keyboard, screen readers, Android, contrast themes, spacing complete

Session 6 · Reduced motion. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce nothing in the phone transitions or animates (18 boxes → 0), and the Play press loses its 2px drop but keeps its darkening and inset shadow. Every other press is colour only. The roadmap asked whether that is enough feedback; I think it is, so no outline was added. Try Simulate reduced motion above.

Session 7 · Keyboard and focus. Every control is a real <button>: 17 tab stops per phone in reading order, Enter and Space with no script. The focus ring is 2px at a 2px offset, keyboard-only. The bottom buttons sit on the backdrop, and in Ember, Tidal and Olive that backdrop is a mid-tone, so those rings are two-colour (light outline plus a dark band; WCAG technique C40). Measured before and after focus on every control in every theme, the ring is 3.13:1 or better (WCAG 2.4.13). roll and on/off now work: on/off flips the layer and its halos, and roll re-deals that layer's notes.

Session 8 · Screen readers. Each phone is a named region and the track name is a heading. The status bar and cover are hidden from screen readers. Patterns read as "Drums pattern: 9 notes in 16 steps, 4 accented". roll is "Roll Drums". on/off is a switch named "Drums" that reads on or off. Play keeps its swapping Play / Pause name.

Session 9 · Android. The inset control now offers Android sim (24dp status, 48dp three-button nav, the worst case). That closes Session 2's Android question.

Session 10 · Windows contrast themes. In forced colours the notes, guide dots, playhead and halos all vanished, because they are drawn as background colours and shadows. They are now redrawn in the user's own system colours: notes CanvasText, dots and off-layer notes GrayText, halo and lit cell Highlight, plus a ring on the lit cell so it differs by shape too.

Session 11 · Text spacing and zoom. With the WCAG 1.4.12 spacing override the Info card and layer labels overflowed. A fit guard now measures each phone whenever its text changes size: a label that would be cut stacks that phone's layers, an item too wide for the column gets its own line, and (since v15) lines that still don't fit take turns in shared slots instead of being dropped. The name never goes below 28px unless nothing else fits. The gallery page also reflows: no sideways scroll at 200% zoom or at 320px.

Session 12 · Final review. A separate reviewer that had not seen the work checked everything with its own scripts. Its first verdict was not ready, with two AA failures. Non-text contrast: several light-theme notes (as low as 1.4:1), the playhead's beat cells and the lit cell were under 3:1; Session 4 had over-claimed. Those notes are now darker in the same hue, beat cells are 3.1:1 or more, and the lit cell got a dark rim (replaced in v15 by the LED glow). Text spacing: labels were cut and a mood item was clipped sideways; fixed as described above. It also found my Session 9 hit pad broke Session 2's rule, so the buttons are now drawn at 48. The on/off switch now follows the ARIA switch pattern, the button words are 11px, the meta reads as a four-item list, the playhead says when it is paused, and the gallery's grey text was raised to 4.5:1. A second pass by the same reviewer confirmed both blockers fixed and found three small leftovers (a long one-word name breaking mid-word under spacing, the meta reading as one run of words, a few stale doc lines), which are fixed too.

Settled in v15. (1) Off-beat cells and empty-slot dots stay quiet; that is the better design, and they are treated as decorative. (2) Lines no longer drop: they take turns in their usual places. (3) The meta is 11px. (4) Safari, Firefox and a real screen reader wait until the design is locked.

v16 · Light themes done

The notes pop now. In Dawn, Ember, Tidal and Olive the notes had been darkened until they were readable, which made them muddy. A dark dot can't glow on a light panel, so these get their energy from color instead: jewel tones (coral, cobalt, emerald, orchid and violet in Dawn; ruby, violet, vermilion, plum and royal blue in Ember; ocean, sea teal, violet, reef coral and cerulean in Tidal; leaf, bronze, gold, forest and olive in Olive, which stays inside its swatch's family and so gains the least). Each one is close to as saturated as a screen can show at a darkness that still clears 3:1. Every note measures 3.56:1 or better.

Kind to color-blind eyes. The rows alternate between a lighter jewel tone and a deeper one, so two neighboring rows differ in lightness as well as hue. Under simulated red-green color blindness, neighboring rows are now well apart in all four themes; in v15, Bass and Keys were nearly identical in Dawn, Tidal and Olive. Two honest limits: rare blue-yellow color blindness (tritanopia) separates Dawn's neighbors a little less than v15 did, and rows that are not next to each other can still look alike. Every row has its label, so color is never the only cue.

The playhead is a solid lamp. The light-theme lit cell is now one saturated color (the theme's Play color), a thin light ring and a soft glow of the same hue, which is how a note looks when the playhead is on it. There is no pale centre and no dark edge any more. It clears 3:1 everywhere it's measured (3.24:1 or better) and is still taller than its neighbors.

Previous and Next are filled. Their triangles are solid now, matching Play and Pause.

v15 · Design pass done

The playhead glows in the light themes too. (Replaced in v16 by the solid lamp.) The dark rim is gone. In Dawn, Ember, Tidal and Olive the lit cell is a little LED: a hot pale centre, a deep coloured edge and a soft pale-gold aura, and it stands a touch taller than the other cells (every theme's lit cell does now). The deep edge is what keeps it at 3:1 or better against the aura around it.

Every button sinks into the screen. Press anything and it drops (2px for the big keys, 1px for roll and on/off), darkens a touch and its shadow turns inward, like Play. With reduced motion on, it darkens without moving.

Off stays pressed in. When a layer is off, its key stays latched down and darker, so you can see at a glance which layers are off. The word "off" is still there and still easy to read (5.7:1 or better).

Info text is 11px, the iOS and Android minimum, with slightly tighter leading so the cover keeps its size.

Lines take turns instead of disappearing. At big text sizes the top slot alternates "53% Tide · 47% Glade" with "93% enthusiastic · 7% refreshing", and the bottom slot alternates "Bb Mixolydian · 93 bpm" with "6/8 · 8 bars", every 4 seconds, with no animation. Each line always appears in the same place. It holds still while the loop is paused or while a mouse rests on the card. Tap the card and it turns at once, and from then on it stays put and only turns when you tap, until the next loop. Nothing is dropped any more, so the text-spacing gap from v14 is closed.

Platform Compliance Roadmap — Sessions 1–12

How to use this. Each session is a single, focused step. A new DeepSeek session should read the current file, do only that step, verify it against the relevant standard, and end by asking the listed questions before the next session begins. Do not bundle steps.
